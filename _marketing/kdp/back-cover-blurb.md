**Your company bought AI. Your operating model never changed.**

The subscriptions are paid. Somebody ran a pilot. Two years in, three people use three different tools and revenue per employee sits exactly where it was. That's not an AI problem. It's an operating problem.

AI arrives inside a company the way a new employee does: it needs a seat, a scope, and the handoffs that let it do the work. In most companies the work was never designed, so there's no seat to take.

*Co-Intelligent Co-Operation* is the field manual for designing the work first. Find the one constraint worth a sprint, put a dollar figure on it, design the work around it, build the system that fixes it, and do it again, so each sprint makes the next one faster.

If you run a company between 25 and 150 people, and adding people to solve problems has stopped working, start here.

**Stop transforming. Start compounding.**
