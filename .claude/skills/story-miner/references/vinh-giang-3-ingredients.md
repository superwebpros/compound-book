# Vinh Giang's 3 Ingredients Framework

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5HfeNDleTS4

## The Three Ingredients

### 1. Be Specific (5+1 Senses)

Use sensory detail to activate the listener's mirror neurons. For each story, identify what you:

- **Saw** — visual details, scene-setting
- **Heard** — sounds, dialogue, ambient noise
- **Smelled** — environmental smells
- **Tasted** — physical taste or dryness
- **Touched** — tactile sensations, temperature
- **Felt (emotion)** — the internal emotional state

One vivid detail per sense is enough. Specificity beats generality for relatability — "my palms were so sweaty the paper started to wrinkle" beats "I was nervous."

### 2. Relive vs. Report

Reporting is the Wikipedia version of your life. Reliving transports the audience into the moment.

**Reporting:** "Last night I walked on stage. I was nervous. Then I gave my talk."
**Reliving:** "I step onto the stage and the spotlights are so bright I can't see the front row. My hands are shaking so hard the notes are rattling."

Three techniques:
- **Use present tense** — "I'm standing there" not "I was standing there"
- **Use sensory detail** — one vivid detail anchors the moment
- **Let the body join in** — gestures, physicality, embodiment

### 3. Share the Meaning

The meaning is what transforms a campfire tale into something that teaches, inspires, or shifts perspective.

**The bridge phrase:** "The reason I'm telling you this is because..."

The meaning must:
- Connect the story to the audience's world (relevance)
- Make sense as a logical link from the story (not forced)
- Be the actual lesson, not just a restated fact

**Structure:** Story (doorway) -> Meaning (what you lead them into)

Without meaning, you're just telling campfire tales. With it, you're teaching and shifting perspective.

## When Stories Work Best

- 15+ minutes and you want to be memorable and influential: tell a story
- Quick answer needed: give the direct answer
- Mastery is knowing which moment you're in

## Key Insight

You don't need crazy life experiences. Everyday moments are more relatable. What makes a story powerful isn't what happened — it's the perspective you bring and the way you frame it.
