# Co-Intelligent Co-Operation — Writer's Brief

You are rewriting (or drafting) **one chapter** of *Co-Intelligent Co-Operation*, a Quarto book by Jesse Flores and Julie Mann (Compound, 2026). This brief is canon for every chapter agent. Read it in full before writing.

## What this book is

A printed/digital lead-magnet artifact for Compound — the brochure-layer artifact in the brand's "long-form / print" tier. CEOs of $5M–$50M revenue companies running on EOS, Scaling Up, or similar systems are the audience. They have bought AI tools, those tools have produced nothing structural, and they are sitting with the headcount math going the wrong way.

The book sells nothing directly. It teaches the framework. It ends with one CTA: the Clarity Call.

Title: **Co-Intelligent Co-Operation: How to build the Human+AI Company of the Future…Today.**

## Voice — operator-to-operator

The reader is a peer. Same vocabulary as a CEO talking to a CEO. Sharper cadence than a consulting deck. Less hedge, more claim.

- **Direct, Confident, No-BS, Wise, Empowering.** No qualifiers that soften the diagnosis. ("Most companies fail at this" — not "Many companies sometimes encounter difficulties in this area.")
- **You** to the reader. Always. Never "one" or "users." Never "our customers" / "our clients" — they are *operators*.
- **We** for Compound when speaking institutionally.
- **Em-dashes — used freely — are part of the editorial rhythm.** Oxford comma. Periods on full-sentence headlines. No exclamation marks.
- **Sentence case headlines with periods.** Never title case. `Find the real constraint.` not `Find the Real Constraint`.
- **One sentence, one beat — full sentences, not staccato fragments.** Em-dashes do the work bullet lists usually do.
- **Numbers are specific and load-bearing.** "If your largest operational constraint costs $150K+ per year, the first sprint pays for the year." > "Save up to 40% on AI costs."

## Forbidden words and phrases

Never use these. They are buyer-skepticism triggers from every failed AI pitch:

- *transformation, transformative, transform* (the book's audience is exhausted by AI transformation pitches)
- *leverage, synergy, alignment* (the meeting kind is fine)
- *AI-powered, AI-enabled, cutting-edge, revolutionary*
- *augmentation* (use *co-intelligence* instead)
- *hybrid* alone — only inside the locked phrases *Hybrid Accountability Chart* and *Hybrid Org Today*
- *organizational design* in headlines/masthead (tactical-only — fine in body if precise)
- *movement, join us, revolution*
- *Orchestrated Organization* — **retired** vocabulary. Use **Co-Intelligent Company**.
- *AI Maturity Diagnostic, diagnostic call, strategy call, discovery call* — retired. Use **Clarity Call**.
- "Learn more," "Discover," "Find out how," "Schedule a demo" — dead CTA language.
- **Emoji. Anywhere. Never.**

## Canonical vocabulary (use these exactly)

**Tagline:** *Stop transforming. Start compounding.*

**The diagnostic phrase:** *"The reason your AI investments aren't producing returns is an operating-model problem — not a technology problem. Specifically, a Co-Intelligent Company problem."*

**The Equation:** *Co-Intelligence + Rhythm = Compound.* (Parallels EOS's *Vision + Traction = Healthy Organization*.)

### Identity / category

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **The Co-Intelligent Company** | The masthead category — what a member's company *becomes*. Replaces the retired "Orchestrated Organization." |
| **Co-intelligence** | Humans and AI working as **one system, not two parallel tracks**. The integration of human + synthetic. |
| **Co-Intelligent Co-operation** | The relational frame — what humans and AI *do*: working the same problems together. The book's title is built on this term. |
| **Co-Operating Model** | The system frame — the structure that makes Co-Intelligent Co-operation possible: who does what across humans and AI. |

### Framework structure — three distinct words

- **Framework** = the whole six-step shape.
- **Sequence** = the ordered steps inside the framework: **Signal → Source → Design → Build → Deliver → Compound**. The order is the argument.
- **Rhythm** = the cadence — running the framework over and over. Quarterly.
- **Sprint** = one pass through the Sequence on one validated constraint, ~6–8 weeks. (Used in this book as the unit of work; introduced cleanly in Chapter 3.)

**Do NOT use these interchangeably.** Each names a distinct thing.

### The six stages of the Sequence

Always all six, always in order, always written with arrows: **Signal → Source → Design → Build → Deliver → Compound.**

| Stage | What it produces |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | Find the one operational problem worth solving first. Deliverable: a validated constraint statement with a quantified cost. |
| **Source** | Map everything the work actually requires — knowledge, context, judgment. Deliverable: a Knowledge Map. |
| **Design** | Decide who does what. Deliverable: the **Hybrid Accountability Chart** for this sprint's accountability. |
| **Build** | Produce the developer-ready spec; build the agent into the workflow the team already uses. |
| **Deliver** | Ship it inside the org. Train the people. Change the handoffs. Measure the win against Signal's quantified cost. |
| **Compound** | Run the retrospective. Capture the learning. Pick the next constraint. Add infrastructure that accelerates the next sprint. |

**The grouping is Diagnose ⟂ Execute & Compound.** Diagnose = Signal + Source (the work most companies skip). Execute & Compound = Design + Build + Deliver + Compound.

This is **different** from how the previous draft of the book grouped them ("Act One: Signal/Source/Design vs. Act Two: Build/Deliver/Compound"). Use the Diagnose / Execute grouping. Design moves into Execute.

### The Compound Bench — six AI coaching agents

Compound provides one coaching agent per stage of the Sequence. These are distinct from the **agent teams the member builds inside their company**.

- **Signal Agent** — Diagnoses constraint. Walks the user from situation → symptom → constraint → dollar cost. Output: a one-page constraint statement.
- **Source Agent** — Maps knowledge & data. Run before a sprint, or when someone is about to leave. Surfaces tribal knowledge, undocumented decisions, at-risk information.
- **Design Agent** — Allocates Human+AI. Helps draft the work allocation, draw the Hybrid Accountability Chart, scope the sprint.
- **Build Agent** — Specs the build. Produces the developer-ready specification: what the workflow does, what tools it integrates with, what guardrails are required.
- **Deliver Agent** — Runs deployment. Identifies who needs training, what handoffs change, how the result gets measured, the rollback plan.
- **Compound Agent** — Runs the retrospective. Captures the learning, measures the result against the original constraint cost, helps rank the next constraint.

### Roles inside the member's company

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Human Orchestrator** | Sets goals. Designs the workflow. Owns the outcome. Reviews at the goal level, not the task level. Makes the design improvements between sprints. |
| **Agent Coordinator** | Operational layer. Ensures inputs are clean. Reviews outputs at the appropriate frequency. Escalates judgment calls. Monitors the agent team's performance. |

Every agent team has a human supervisor. There are no unowned teams.

### Key artifacts (named instruments inside the member's company)

| Artifact | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Hybrid Accountability Chart** | An org chart designed for a human + AI workforce. Defines which tasks stay with people, which move to agents, who owns each handoff. Output of the Design stage. |
| **The Hybrid Org Today** | The living document every member maintains: what people own, what AI handles, what's solved, what's next. Updated quarterly during the Rhythm. The Hybrid Accountability Chart is one slice of the Hybrid Org Today. |

### Pain framing

- **The Headcount Paradox** — the dynamic everyone feels but nobody has named: companies keep adding headcount without changing outcomes.
- **Headcount math** — the vernacular ("the math isn't working"). Use this in copy that opens with pain.
- **Design Before Deploy** — the Compound philosophy: organizational design must precede technology deployment. The Sequence is the argument.

## Format and output rules

- **Output a single complete Quarto markdown (.qmd) file** at the path your chapter-specific brief gives you.
- **Headings:** sentence case with periods on complete sentences. `# Chapter Title.` not `# Chapter Title`. Subheadings: `## Section title.`
- **Emphasis in headings** = wrap the load-bearing word in `*asterisks*`. The book's CSS renders heading `<em>` as a red rule-underline. Body text `*emphasis*` renders as standard italic — fine for body, do not overuse.
- **Em-dashes** are the editorial rhythm — use them freely.
- **The arrow** `→` (Unicode U+2192) for the Sequence: `Signal → Source → Design → Build → Deliver → Compound`. Never `->`.
- **No emoji.** Anywhere.
- **No bullet lists for narrative content.** Em-dashes carry that load. Reserve bullets and numbered lists for genuine enumerations (the four questions of the Hybrid Accountability Chart; the six stages; etc.).
- **Tables** where they add clarity (e.g., the Human/Agent split in the Co-Operating Model chapter).
- **Stories** embed as named sidebars at the natural moment in the chapter — `### Sprint · {topic}` or just inline narrative. Keep each story to 100–250 words. Specific, declarative, dollar/time figures where they exist.

## Structure expectations per chapter

Each chapter should:

1. Open with a single declarative paragraph that names what this chapter is about and why it matters. Not a throat-clear.
2. Move through 3–6 sections, each with a sentence-case period-terminated H2.
3. Include the embedded story (or stories) at the natural moment, not dumped at the end.
4. Close with a clear handoff to what comes next — usually one paragraph, sometimes a one-line transition.
5. **Never end on a Conclusion section that recaps what was just said.** Trust the reader.
