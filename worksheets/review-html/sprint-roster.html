Name the Sprint roster

Produces a three-line roster — Orchestrator, Owners, Builder — that lands directly on your Hybrid Org Today and confirms your first Sprint is fully staffed before it starts.

This worksheet turns "we should put someone on this" into three named people. You'll name the one person who owns the constraint outcome and supervises the agent team, the two or three people closest to the problem every day, and whoever will actually build the solution. The discipline is naming real people, not titles or "TBD." Get one constraint from your minimum viable Signal first — the roster is staffed around a specific constraint, not in the abstract. No engineer or org chart required; you need to know who owns the work and who can build.

1. Identify the Human Orchestrator

Assigns workflow ownership and agent-supervision authority to one named person before the Sprint starts.

  1. Answer one question: who owns the workflow where the constraint lives? Write that person's name. This is not a technical role and not a new hire — it's the operator who already owns the outcome and can judge whether the agent's output is right.
  2. Confirm they can actually supervise the agent team, not just sponsor it. They need to be close enough to the work to catch a wrong answer, and to own the result either way.
  3. If you run EOS, picture the Integrator role scoped down to one workflow: the Orchestrator runs the Sprint the way the Integrator runs the company. Record the name where it will land — the Human Orchestrator row on the Active Sprint of your Hybrid Org Today.
Meridian example: Elena Ruiz (VP of Operations) was the Human Orchestrator. She owned quoting, knew the exceptions, and could judge whether a draft quote was right — so she could supervise the agent, not just sign off on it.

2. Name the daily-proximate supervisors

Identifies the two or three people closest to the problem who contribute to Signal and Source and catch what reports miss.

  1. Answer: who are the two or three people closest to the problem daily? Name them. These are the people in the room for Signal and Source — they see what the reports don't show.
  2. For each one, write the one sentence that explains why they're on the list — what they uniquely see that a dashboard or a manager two steps removed would miss.
  3. Hold the list to two or three. These are the people who populate the supervisor column of your Hybrid Accountability Chart, not the whole department.
Meridian example: Ty Banfield (Sales Lead) and Dave Kowalski (Senior Design Engineer) were the daily-proximate supervisors. Ty saw the quotes the way customers saw them; Dave knew which specs the agent would struggle with.

3. Confirm the builder resource

Locks in who will construct the solution — internal developer, external partner, or Compound membership — so the Sprint is fully staffed.

  1. Answer: do you have someone who can build? Name the resource and the type — internal developer, external partner, or Compound membership. "We'll figure it out" is not a builder.
  2. Note the rough scope you're committing — you don't need a hire or an engineering team, just enough builder time to construct this one solution.
  3. Record it on the Builder row. With the Orchestrator and supervisors named, this confirms the Sprint is fully staffed and nothing waits on a person you haven't found yet.
Meridian example: A freelance n8n developer — three days of API work — was the builder. No new hires, no internal engineering team. That was the full roster: one Orchestrator, two supervisors, one freelance builder.

Before you call it done

You're done when someone who wasn't in the room could read the roster and know exactly who runs the Sprint, who's in the room for Signal, and who builds — without asking you a single question. Check:

  • All three roles hold a real named person, not a title and not "TBD." A blank or a placeholder means the Sprint isn't staffed.
  • The Human Orchestrator owns the outcome and can judge the agent's work — not just sponsor it from a distance.
  • The supervisors are two or three people who are close to the problem daily, each with a one-line reason they see what reports miss.
  • The builder is named and the type is settled — internal, external, or Compound — so nothing stalls on finding one.
  • The three names are recorded where the Sprint lives: the Active Sprint row and supervisor column of your Hybrid Org Today.
