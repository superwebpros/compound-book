List the sources.

01

Every task in the constraint workflow consumes information from somewhere and sends it somewhere else. List every digital source that touches it, then mark which layer it lives in. The layer tells Design what exists to work with — and what has to be built before any agent can reason reliably.

System of RecordAuthoritative data. CRM, ERP, QuickBooks, the job-costing database. If it's wrong here, it's wrong everywhere.
System of KnowledgeInstitutional know-how. SOPs, playbooks, pricing policy, the doc that explains how exceptions actually work.
System of SemanticsMeaning & relationships. What ties "Client A" in the CRM to "Account 0042" in the ERP. Most companies don't have one yet — note it as a gap.
  1. 1.One row per source: shared drives, inboxes, spreadsheets, the CRM, the ERP, the process logs.
  2. 2.Tag the layer and name a real owner. "Nobody" is a finding, not a blank.
  3. 3.Mark status: Clean (usable as-is), Needs Work (quality/format problems), or Missing (should exist, doesn't).
SourceLayerOwnerStatus
Watch for — the data-audit trap

If every row is a System of Record and none is Knowledge or Semantics, you ran a data audit, not Source. The know-how that makes the constraint hard usually lives in the Knowledge layer — or in nobody's system at all. Keep it scoped to the one constraint; if it doesn't touch the workflow, it doesn't go on the map.

Run the pipeline audit.

02

Knowing where data lives isn't enough — Design needs to know whether it moves. For each source, ask: does it connect to the other systems around the constraint, or is a person carrying data by hand? Mark each one Connected, Manual, or Broken.

APIHow systems talk to systems. Your CRM has one; your ERP has one. Other software can ask it questions and get structured answers back.
MCPHow AI talks to your systems. Lets a model read your CRM or check a status live — the difference between a chatbot and a workflow.
ConnectorPre-built integration. Zapier, Make, n8n — links two systems without custom code. The thing companies buy before doing this work.
  1. 1.Connected — an API, MCP, or connector already carries data between this source and the systems around it.
  2. 2.Manual — a person is carrying data a connection could carry (export, re-key, copy-paste).
  3. 3.Broken — the data doesn't move at all; it sits in one place nobody can reach programmatically. Every Manual or Broken row is your handoff to Design.
SourceConnected / Manual / BrokenLast verified accurate?
Pro tip

You don't need to understand the engineering behind APIs or MCPs. You need to know whether your systems talk to each other or a person is carrying data between them. That's the whole question this column answers — and it's what you hand a contractor or leave for Design to wire.

Tier it for AI.

03

Each source is reachable by AI today, reachable with extraction work, or not reachable at all. The tier tells Design what to use directly, what to build a pipeline for, and what stays human. Run it while the map is fresh — it goes fast.

Tier 1 — use directly · structured & API-accessible Tier 2 — extract first · unstructured but capturable Tier 3 — human judgment · not AI-accessible yet Not AI — ephemeral / in-the-moment
  1. 1.Tag each source 1, 2, 3, or Not AI. Also note structured vs. unstructured.
  2. 2.An AI workflow that mixes Tier 1 truth with a stale Tier 3 archive will confidently cite your 2019 pricing. The tiers exist to prevent exactly that.
  3. 3.A valuable Tier 2 source (a transcript, a PDF of SOPs) is a Design decision: how much structure to add, and where.

Why this matters downstream

Agents treat every accessible piece of information as equally valid unless you tell them otherwise. A human handed bad data slows down and asks a question. An agent handed bad data executes against it, at scale, every time. The tier and the status are how you stop that before Build.

Name what's missing.

04

The first three steps describe what exists. This column reveals the most risk: knowledge a designer would need that isn't represented anywhere — questions your current sources can't answer, decisions made by gut today that should be made by data. Every missing source is a potential Sprint failure mode. Name them before Build, not after deployment finds them.

  1. 1.List every question a designer would ask that your current sources can't answer.
  2. 2.For each, note what it should be — a digital system, a documented process, captured expertise — and what it'd take to create.
  3. 3.If the Missing column is empty, you didn't look hard enough. Zero gaps is a red flag, not a clean bill.

Worked example — Meridian's two missing inputs
