# AI Readiness Scorecard: Where Your Company Actually Stands

**Chapter:** 01 — Diagnosis
**Artifact produced:** Completed AI Readiness Scorecard (five dimensions, scored Red/Yellow/Green)
**Who's in the room:** Leadership team — whoever would be in the room if you were deciding whether to invest in AI

---

## Purpose

Most companies skip diagnosis entirely. They buy tools, hire consultants, and launch pilots without ever asking the honest question: are we actually ready to operate differently? This scorecard forces that question. You score five dimensions of operational readiness, and the pattern that emerges tells you where to start — or whether to start at all.

---

## Part 1: Score the Five Dimensions

For each dimension, read the three descriptions and circle the one that matches your company right now. Not where you want to be. Not where you were last quarter. Right now.

### Dimension 1: Constraint Clarity

*Can your leadership team name the ONE operational constraint AI should solve?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **Red** | No clear constraint. The team talks about AI in general terms — "we should use AI" — but cannot name a specific operational problem it would solve. |
| **Yellow** | Multiple candidates, no priority. The issues list has several items that "could benefit from AI," but the team has not ranked them or committed to one. |
| **Green** | One validated constraint with a cost. The team can name the constraint in one sentence and attach a dollar figure to it. |

> **Pro Tip:** If your leadership team can't agree on the constraint, that disagreement IS the diagnosis. It means priorities aren't aligned. No AI tool fixes misaligned priorities.

> **Action Step:** Poll your leadership team individually — not in a group. Ask each person: "What is the ONE operational problem AI should solve for us?" Write down their answers verbatim. If you get five different answers from five people, you have your diagnosis.

### Dimension 2: Information Readiness

*Is the knowledge your team runs on documented and accessible, or trapped in people's heads?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **Red** | Mostly tribal. The way things get done lives in the heads of a few key people. When they're out, things slow down or break. |
| **Yellow** | Partially documented. Some processes are written down, but the critical knowledge — pricing logic, client history, escalation paths — still depends on specific individuals. |
| **Green** | Documented and indexed. Processes, decisions, and institutional knowledge are written down, stored in systems the team can access, and maintained regularly. |

```
YOUR SCORE: [ Red / Yellow / Green ]

NOTES:
_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________
```

> **Pro Tip:** Here's the test: if your best operations person quit tomorrow, how long before the replacement could run the workflow without calling them? If the answer is "months," you're Red regardless of what's in your wiki.

> **Action Step:** Pick your most critical workflow. Ask the person who owns it: "If you had to hand this off to someone new on Monday, what would you give them?" Whatever they describe is the actual state of your documentation — not whatever lives in SharePoint.

---

### Dimension 3: Workflow Visibility

*Can you draw the workflow where the constraint lives, including who does what and where handoffs break?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **Red** | Nobody can draw it. The workflow exists as a series of habits and reactions. If you asked three people to map it, you'd get three different maps. |
| **Yellow** | Someone knows it but it's not documented. One or two people could walk you through the workflow, but it lives in their heads and has never been drawn, validated, or shared. |
| **Green** | Documented with ownership. The workflow is mapped, each step has an owner, handoffs are identified, and the team agrees on where it breaks. |

```
YOUR SCORE: [ Red / Yellow / Green ]

NOTES:
_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________
```

> **Pro Tip:** The gap between Yellow and Green is not documentation — it's agreement. A workflow that one person can describe is a story. A workflow the team has validated is a map. AI needs the map.

> **Action Step:** Whiteboard your most constrained workflow in 15 minutes. Put every handoff on the board. Circle the ones where work stalls, errors appear, or people say "I'll just handle it myself." Those circles are where the real problem lives.

---

### Dimension 4: Decision Rights

*Does your team know who decides what AI handles vs. what stays with humans?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **Red** | No conversation has happened. The team has not discussed which tasks AI could take over, which tasks require human judgment, and who makes that call. |
| **Yellow** | Vague ideas. People have opinions about what AI "should" do, but there are no explicit assignments, no written boundaries, and no agreement on who decides. |
| **Green** | Explicit assignments per role. The team has defined which decisions and tasks are AI-eligible, which stay human, and who has authority to change the boundary. |

```
YOUR SCORE: [ Red / Yellow / Green ]

NOTES:
_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________
```

> **Pro Tip:** Decision rights aren't about AI capability. They're about organizational trust. The question isn't "Can AI do this?" — it's "Do we trust the output enough to act on it without a human checking?" That's a leadership decision, not a technology decision.

> **Action Step:** List the five most frequent decisions in your constrained workflow. For each one, write down who currently makes the decision and whether it requires judgment or just follows a rule. The rule-based ones are AI-eligible. The judgment ones need a human-in-the-loop design.

---

### Dimension 5: Measurement Discipline

*Can you put a dollar number on what the constraint costs per quarter?*

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **Red** | Can't quantify. The team knows something is expensive or slow, but cannot produce a number. No one tracks the cost of the constraint. |
| **Yellow** | Rough estimate. Someone can say "it probably costs us around $X" but the number isn't validated, isn't tracked, and wouldn't survive scrutiny. |
| **Green** | Validated number with data. The cost is calculated from real data — hours tracked, margin analyzed, revenue measured — and the team can show the math. |

```
YOUR SCORE: [ Red / Yellow / Green ]

NOTES:
_____________________________________________________________
_____________________________________________________________
```

> **Pro Tip:** If you can't measure what the constraint costs today, you can't measure whether AI made it better tomorrow. Measurement discipline is the difference between an AI initiative and an AI experiment. Experiments are optional. Initiatives have ROI targets.

> **Action Step:** Attempt the math right now. Hours per week on the constraint, multiplied by loaded cost per hour, multiplied by 13 weeks. That's your quarterly cost floor. If you can't fill in the numbers, write down what data you'd need and where you'd get it.

## Part 2: Read the Pattern

Fill in your overall scorecard:

```
DIMENSION                 SCORE
---------------------------------------
Constraint Clarity        [ R / Y / G ]
Information Readiness     [ R / Y / G ]
Workflow Visibility       [ R / Y / G ]
Decision Rights           [ R / Y / G ]
Measurement Discipline    [ R / Y / G ]
```

### What the pattern tells you:

- **All Red:** You are not ready for an AI initiative. You are ready for this book. The Compound Sprint framework exists to move you from Red to Green, dimension by dimension.
- **Mixed Red/Yellow:** Start with whichever dimension scored Yellow. Yellow means there's something to work with — a partial answer, an incomplete artifact, a person who knows but hasn't written it down. One Yellow is more actionable than five Reds.
- **Mostly Yellow:** You're closer than you think. The work is clarification and commitment, not creation from scratch. The Sprint framework will move fast for you.
- **Any Green:** Protect it. A Green score means someone did the work to document, quantify, or decide. Build from that foundation — don't let a new initiative overwrite the discipline that produced it.
- **All Green:** You don't need the diagnosis chapter. Skip to Chapter 4 (Signal) and run the Sprint.

## Part 3: Populated Example

### Company Profile

**Company:** Meridian Consulting Group — $5M revenue, 28 people, professional services (management consulting and fractional ops). Runs EOS. Two partners, one ops manager, four project leads.

### Their Scorecard

```
DIMENSION                 SCORE     REASONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Constraint Clarity        Yellow    Issues list has 12 items. L10
                                    meetings keep circling three of them.
                                    Nobody has said "this is the ONE."
                                    Partners disagree on priority.

Information Readiness     Red       The ops manager IS the documentation.
                                    She runs onboarding from memory,
                                    manages client delivery timelines in
                                    her head, and is the only person who
                                    knows how the invoicing logic works.
                                    When she took two weeks off, three
                                    deliverables slipped.

Workflow Visibility       Yellow    Project leads know their own client
                                    workflows but can't describe each
                                    other's. The handoff from sales to
                                    delivery is different every time.
                                    Someone could draw it — but the
                                    drawings would conflict.

Decision Rights           Red       The AI conversation has not happened.
                                    One partner bought a ChatGPT Team
                                    license. Three people use it. Nobody
                                    has discussed what it should or
                                    shouldn't be used for. No policy,
                                    no guidelines, no boundaries.

Measurement Discipline    Yellow    They know the ops manager bottleneck
                                    is expensive. One partner estimated
                                    "probably $150K in lost capacity."
                                    But the number isn't calculated —
                                    it's a guess from a gut feeling.
                                    Nobody tracks hours lost to the
                                    bottleneck.
```

### What the pattern tells Meridian

Two Reds, three Yellows. The Yellows are where to start. Constraint Clarity is Yellow because they have candidates — they just haven't committed. Workflow Visibility is Yellow because people know the workflows — they just haven't agreed on one version. Measurement Discipline is Yellow because someone has an estimate — it just needs validation.

The Reds are the deeper problems. Information Readiness is Red because one person IS the system — and AI can't augment a system that doesn't exist outside someone's head. Decision Rights is Red because the conversation hasn't started — and until it does, any AI tool they buy is a toy, not an investment.

Meridian's starting point: lock the constraint (turn Constraint Clarity from Yellow to Green), then map the workflow around it (turn Workflow Visibility from Yellow to Green). Those two moves create the foundation for everything that follows.

## Summary of Action Steps

1. **Poll your leadership team individually** — ask each person to name the ONE constraint AI should solve. Compare answers. (Dimension 1)
2. **Test your documentation** — ask your most critical workflow owner what they'd hand off to a replacement on Monday. (Dimension 2)
3. **Whiteboard the constrained workflow** — 15 minutes, every handoff, circle where work stalls. (Dimension 3)
4. **List the five most frequent decisions** — determine which are rule-based (AI-eligible) and which require judgment (human-in-the-loop). (Dimension 4)
5. **Do the math** — hours per week on the constraint, times loaded cost, times 13 weeks. Write the number or write down what data you'd need. (Dimension 5)
6. **Read the pattern** — fill in the overall scorecard and identify your starting dimension.
