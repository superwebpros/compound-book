## Chapter syllogism

- **Premise 1.** The reader has scored themselves on Chapter 1's five dimensions and committed to running a Sprint against the lowest one.
- **Premise 2.** Every Sprint requires ONE validated constraint with a dollar number attached. Not three, not "significant" — one constraint, one number.
- **Premise 3.** Symptoms aren't constraints. Tracing a symptom to its structural root is a facilitated discipline (Five Whys + constraint questions + tiebreaker rubric), not a worksheet a single operator fills out alone.
- **Conclusion.** The reader leaves Signal with a one-page Constraint Statement: one sentence naming function / blocked outcome / structural cause / cost, with a dollar number attached and the room's agreement that it names the right problem.

- D. **Picking among the survivors — tiebreaker rubric** *(L174-184)*

  **Heading (proposed):** "Pick Among the Survivors"
  *(was H3 `### Picking among candidates.` — directive)*

  - **Assumes:** the trace step left multiple candidates standing.
  - **Establishes:** the five-criteria rubric (foundation first → highest pain inside reach → smallest gap → owner in the room → two-quarter horizon) applied in order; the first candidate passing all five is where the Sprint starts.
  - **Sets up:** the chosen candidate goes into the Five Constraint Questions (G). Meridian (E) shows the rubric working.

    - 1. **Foundation first.** Some candidates unblock the others. Constraint Clarity is almost always the foundation. If a candidate is a precondition for the others, it goes first.
    - 2. **Highest pain inside reach.** Pick the candidate closest to the operational pain that brought you to the conversation. Not the most interesting, not the easiest — the one on the workflow that's actually hurting.
    - 3. **Smallest gap to resolution.** When two candidates are tied on pain, pick the one with the smallest distance to a locked constraint. Less distance = faster lock = credibility for the harder work behind it.
    - 4. **Owner in the room.** Don't pick a candidate nobody on the leadership team owns. If you can't name the person who'll move it, you've picked a wish, not a constraint.
    - 5. **Two-quarter horizon.** Pick what you can plausibly lock and Sprint inside two quarters with the team you've got. Anything longer is too big and needs to be broken into a tighter constraint first.
    - 6. Apply the rubric in order. The first candidate that passes all five is where you start.

  - **Assumes:** reader has run the five questions on their candidate(s) and has a quantified cost.
